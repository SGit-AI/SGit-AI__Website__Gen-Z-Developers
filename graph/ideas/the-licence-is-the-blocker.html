Twice now the product was possible and the licence was the blocker: read the terms, not only the docs

Every technical piece for renting a Mac to an agent by the minute exists, and Apple's macOS licence forbids leasing a Mac for less than twenty-four consecutive hours, and only for developer services. OpenRouter's Management API documents 'unique API keys for each customer instance' with credit limits, and section 7(4) of its terms (31 August 2026) forbids 'reselling API access to Models', so a plan built against the docs was dead on the page that documented it. Before a founder builds the commercial model on a provider, the terms are the thing to read, and an enterprise or OEM agreement is a door to ask about, not a feature to assume.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. The cost of the licence, from the desktop article, at prices read on 7 October 2026: 44 hours of work a month costs about $7.29 on an E2B or Daytona Linux sandbox, $17.60 on Windows 365 for Agents, 116.16 euros on a Scaleway Mac mini M4, $649.44 on an AWS M4 and $1,040.16 on an M4 Pro, because each day is a 24-hour lease. Quote: 'The Mac is not expensive per hour; it is expensive because the hour you can buy is a day.' Both articles say they are read, not interpreted, and are not legal advice; the book should say the same. The nearest fit found for the capped key was ElevenLabs: per-key credit limits, OEM terms under which a business sets its own price for its end users, and a grants programme (twelve months, for companies under 25 people with a monetised use).
