Client-side encryption reduces the exposure under GDPR; it does not take you out of it

Dinis's memo said that with the data encrypted you do not have to worry about GDPR, and the article corrects it: for whoever holds the keys, encrypted personal data is still personal data, storing it is still processing, and the controller's duties stay (the UK regulator says not to think encrypted data is outside the UK GDPR). What encryption does is reduce the exposure, and the law recognises it: Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and under Article 34(3)(a) a breach need not be communicated to the people affected if the data was made unintelligible to anyone not authorised, provided the key was not lost with it. The Court of Justice held in September 2025 that pseudonymised data is not personal data in all cases and for every person, which may help a store that never has the keys. Much less to worry about, not nothing.
