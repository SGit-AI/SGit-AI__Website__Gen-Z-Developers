A startup should double down on understandability: less code, the same tools, and it can be checked and pass

The test is risk-based, not pure. A startup that says it generated most of its code fast, that the product is not mission-critical and here are the mitigations has passed; what fails is not knowing. So build the graph of your own repository, keep the threat model with the code, write a behaviour policy for every agent, trace bugs to changes, and answer the buyer's questions in the buyer's format before being asked. The startup can play the same game as the biggest buyer, with open source tools, and it has less code: understanding ten thousand lines is a week, ten million that nobody still at the company wrote is a programme. The incumbent's advantage was that nobody could check; the startup's is that it can be checked and pass.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. A threat model is the fastest read of a team's understanding: one that says 'we have a weakness here, exploiting it needs a nation-state, we will not defend against it' is a good one. The evidence that review is being skipped is dated in the article: AI accounts for 42% of committed code and only 48% always verify it (Sonar, January 2026); 80% of PRs have no human comment when an AI review tool is on (Qodo, 2025).
