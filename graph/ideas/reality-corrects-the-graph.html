A graph is trustworthy not because it is right but because it is where users, experts and tests can correct it

A model's first reading of anything will be partly wrong; the plan does not need it right. It needs layers someone knows and predictions something can check: users confirm the top, experts their own layer, tests the execution, and every correction is a commit with a name on it. Patterns in the shape are findings in themselves.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. 'Replicate reality first, then have a fact-based conversation': derive what a parser can derive, deterministically, with a file and a line for every node; let a model propose what a parser cannot see and mark every proposal as proposed; then confront the graph with users, experts and tests. The order for anything built now: brief, plan, architecture, review of the architecture, simulation with synthetic users, code last; the review is the join between the projected graph and the derived one, with three outcomes (match, derived not projected, projected not derived).
