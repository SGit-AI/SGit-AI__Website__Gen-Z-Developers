Models do not separate code from data, so a rule in the prompt can be argued with by an email

In a computer the instructions and the data they operate on are kept apart, and most of the worst security bugs in history, from buffer overflows to SQL injection, are what happens when that separation breaks; in a language model there is no separation to break, every instruction and every piece of content arrives in the same channel, and the UK's National Cyber Security Centre said in December 2025 that prompt injection may never be mitigated the way SQL injection was. A behaviour policy does not fix this; it moves the most important instructions, what the agent may do, into a channel the content cannot reach, loaded by the platform, versioned, signed and checked outside the model where it can be.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. Sits beside secrets-never-in-the-chat (the lethal trifecta) and is the reason mandate-reach-gap calls a rule in a prompt hope. Quote worth keeping, Simon Willison's: 'LLMs follow instructions in content'.
