Billing by not building billing: the commercial model is a property of the key

A user pays five pounds through a payment link; the company keeps its cut at the moment of payment; the rest becomes the credit limit on a key handed to the user; the provider meters it and the key stops when it is spent. Against a billing system (a meter on every call, a ledger per user, disputes, refunds, fraud, custody of the data while it meters) that is a payment, a cut, a key with a ceiling and nothing else, and it is more profitable per pound because the margin is taken once, up front. The minimum top-up is five pounds because a card fee with a fixed component makes a one-pound purchase return about 59 pence of inference.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. Could also sit in price-it-then-give-it-away. The model was written down on 28 July 2026, the day the product started, in a brief headed 'Billing by not building billing'. It is why a router like OpenRouter does well: it sells credit, keeps a margin and meters for everyone else. The user owns the ceiling: a key with a limit cannot cost more than the limit, and the user can revoke it at the provider in one click.
