A graph is a network of nodes and edges, not a diagram

Some readers think a graph is a picture with boxes; the book means a network, nodes and the edges between them, and shows it with visualisations. Of the three things people mean by the word (networks for analysis, graph databases for storage, semantics for meaning) the book uses the first and the third.

Decided by. Dinis, in Everything is connected; the editor records it in the vault

Where it comes from

  • Everything is connected A graph is not a diagram (article)
  • The graphs books 'In this book a graph is a network: nodes and the edges that connect them. Never a chart, never a figure'; 'A node connected to nothing is literally meaningless' (newsroom)
