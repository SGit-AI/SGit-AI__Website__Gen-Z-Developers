Pay after the value: credit first, a meter, a balance that can go below zero, and no subscription by default

Most purchases are bets on future value, and the bet is often lost: gym members on a flat fee of over $70 attended 4.3 times a month and paid more than $17 a visit when a $10-a-visit pass was on offer. Turn it round where you can: start the user with credit, charge for the share they actually use, let the balance go negative, and ask for payment when the value is in view. A subscription is a discount for regular use, not rent on something somebody ignores; do not reach for one by default. The condition is a very large gap between the cost of serving and what you can charge, which a static site on a CDN has in the extreme and a business with a real cost per unit does not. Make it fair by country, visible in where the money goes, and chosen at more than one level.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. The newsroom's meter: everyone starts with £5.00 of credit; 10p for a new article read to the end, 5p for an older one, only as far as read; the balance can go below zero and nothing is blocked. The article says there are no results yet and the review is on 6 December 2026; the gym, theatre and photo studies explain the psychology, not the newsroom's results. The reader (read/) on this site runs by the same rules.
