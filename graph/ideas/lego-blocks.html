Building blocks: cloud as Lego, free models with your own key, git as a standard

What a non-specialist can assemble today, mostly free, with an agent doing the one-time setup; serverless as ephemeral town planner services; knowing what tokens cost.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. Costs for a newcomer is still a gap. The four properties serverless always meant to Dinis: it scales to zero, it costs little or nothing when it is not there, it starts fast, and it turns a capability into a commodity. The three words of the name each cover a part the others miss: ephemeral (comes and goes with use, costs nothing when gone), town planner (Wardley's word: mature, industrialised, a commodity, not a prototype held together behind the scenes), services (exposed for others to use, usually through the web). Lambda is an ephemeral town planner service sitting on town planner services like S3, which are always there. The name is a proposal; Dinis does not expect it to replace serverless. The free limits as Cloudflare's pages gave them on 18 October 2026 (they change): static assets free and unlimited on every plan; Workers 100,000 requests a day and 10 ms of CPU each, $5 a month for 10 million; R2 10 GB, a million writes and ten million reads a month, no egress fees, buckets pinnable to the EU; D1 5 GB; KV 100,000 reads and 1,000 writes a day; Durable Objects 100,000 requests a day and 5 GB; Workers AI 10,000 neurons a day then $0.011 per 1,000, 71 models on Cloudflare's own GPUs with a promise not to train on what you send; Email Routing free and unlimited; Access free to 50 users; domains at the registry's price. If staying inside is the point, the model name is the control: a @cf/ model stays on Cloudflare's GPUs, anything else leaves through AI Gateway. The catch for a non-specialist is setup, because Cloudflare's API cannot be called from a web page: a Deploy to Cloudflare button, a temporary account (live for an hour, since June), or an agent with the command line, once. Five packages, each the smallest set for one job and each on the free plan: a site on my domain; my vault, hosted; an assistant over my documents; an inbox that does things; a form or a small API. Each is a repository with a button, a skill and a page saying exactly what it can reach, which is its behaviour policy. The command line is changing: cf entered open beta on 28 September and will replace Wrangler, maintained for 18 months after the beta ends.
