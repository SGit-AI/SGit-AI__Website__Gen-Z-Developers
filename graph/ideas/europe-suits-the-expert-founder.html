Europe's slower, sceptical money suits a founder who can build first

European investors are said to want to see it working, and the data has a version of it: European companies raise at higher revenue (a median band of $1 million to $5 million against $100,000 to $1 million in the US), at lower pre-money valuations ($6.54 million against $14.64 million), with funding targets 46% smaller. That scepticism is what an expert founder now needs, because money too soon is one of the worst things that can happen: 74% of high-growth internet startups fail from premature scaling and investors put in 2 to 3 times more than needed in the discovery phase. Europe is weak at scaling (5% of global VC funds raised against 52% in the US; close to 30% of unicorns founded in Europe 2008 to 2021 moved abroad), and strong at the first part, which is the part that now matters most. Its values fit too: an open source strategy in the Commission's June 2026 sovereignty package, and impact at 15% of European VC against 7% in North America.

Decided by. Nobody yet: a candidate for Dinis to read, comment on and decide (keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it).

Note. The article calls the Europe and US comparison weakly evidenced: one vendor's aggregated data (Equidam), no clean Series A study. Could also serve the-company-itself (the EU) or civic-responsibility (judo for small countries); placed with funding because the argument is about when money arrives.
