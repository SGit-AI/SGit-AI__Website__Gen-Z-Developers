Release history

Every release of this site, narrated: a reader should understand the change without opening the diff. Every push to dev is a release, tagged vX.Y.Z by CI, which refuses a release whose version.txt disagrees with the commit subject.

v0.2.172026-10-19Storyboards you can process quickly, and faces that read the way they were meant to. Dinis asked for the animation to be optional, for a way to see the panels as a flow rather than one at a time, and said the Guide looked snarky and Tomás nearly always sad. The faces: the eyebrows were offset the same way on both eyes, so 'determined', 'worried' and 'thinking' drew one brow up and one down (a sardonic look) and 'happy' drew a frown under a smile; with the lopsided 'determined' mouth that was the snarky Guide, and 'thinking', Tomás's commonest mood, came out as a frown. Brows are now mirrored, the mouths are symmetric, and there are four new moods (excited, calm, sad, confused). The views: every storyboard opens as a deck, a grid (every panel on one page, one to six per row, each panel's text under it or beside it, a click plays a panel, Play in order plays them one after another) or a script (the storyboard as text: who is in each panel, their pose and mood, what they say, the evidence, the note and the source), with a filter and keys for everything. Animation is a setting, kept in the viewer's browser only; with it off a panel is simply there and Play shows its arrival on demand. New pages: every panel of every storyboard on one page (/storyboards/all.html), and the model sheet (/storyboards/model-sheet.html), every character in every mood and pose, to judge a drawing once rather than panel by panel. The night setting is now night. The build refuses an unknown setting or prop, which used to draw as nothing. storyboard_png.py no longer lets the site's sticky navigation cover the top of each panel, and --grid writes a whole storyboard as one contact-sheet image.
v0.2.162026-10-19Two storyboard fixes Dinis asked for. The flick on moving forward: a panel used to arrive fully drawn and then blank and redraw itself when its play began; now a panel arrives as blank paper, plays once the first time it is reached, and stays drawn after that, going forward or back. Full screen on an iPad: Safari's prefixed full-screen styles were missing and the request can fail silently; the deck now uses the prefixed selectors, and where full screen is refused or absent it pins itself to the viewport with the same button (and Escape) to leave.

v0.2.142026-10-18Article 14, Lenses, roles and loops, from Dinis's memo on how LLMs and agents work, co-written in his voice: a prompt is a lens (translating into another language and for a teenager, a musician, a doctor or a football fan are the same act to a model; 'bad at maths' is often the wrong lens); a role is a model with a description and a question; an agent is a role on a loop with a goal; tools are requests the model sends back, which the harness runs and resends with the next request, with the dates from ChatGPT to the Model Context Protocol; the model remembers nothing, hence the context window; long chats are compressed into lower-resolution summaries (the newsroom's eighteen summaries at 1.7%); so many focused, named threads, as in the email workflow. A 'Precisely' section makes the technical points exact (every request is independent, the model never runs a tool, memory features are still the context window). Storyboard 10, thirteen panels: five lenses on one idea, a role as a graph, the agent loop with one tool call, what the next request carries, the timeline, the compression bars, the named threads; every Guide line and quote card checked against the article by the build. The editor's brief.

v0.2.82026-10-18What the book means by generative AI. A co-written article, generative AI, not AI: AI is a huge bucket and the book is about the generative kind; Dinis's picture of how it works (a model builds its own representation and generates from it; a language model predicts the next word from a rich picture of how things connect, which is why a better prompt helps and why hallucinations happen; the image-extension tools of 2022 and 2023 that made it click; the patterns models found in our language; the wonder of what they can still turn out to do); and, because he asked to be corrected, a section of corrections with sources: classic machine learning learns too, the difference is predicting versus generating; inside a model there are vectors and attention, not a stored graph, so the graph is a metaphor; the prompt conditions rather than fine-tunes; next-token prediction plus training on feedback; a second cause of hallucinations from training that rewards guessing (Kalai and others, September 2025); diffusion for most image models; emergent abilities debated (Wei 2022, Schaeffer 2023). A note on the name (Gen Z, Gen AI, Gen Alpha born 2010 to 2024) left for the title interview. A 12-panel storyboard, quote-checked by the build, and a brief for the editor.
