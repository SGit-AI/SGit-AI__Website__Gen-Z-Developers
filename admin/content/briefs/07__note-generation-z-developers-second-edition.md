## The ask

A second edition of Dinis's 2018 book *Generation Z Developers*: the manual he would hand to people starting out, for getting into development and getting a job, now that everyone who can brief an agent is a developer. It is the first book to make this way, before the graphs books and a new Hacking Portugal. Keep the title and date the edition October 2026; frame it with Wardley's explorers, villagers and town planners, and explain Wardley maps; finish the outlines from the newsroom's articles; say what changed; lots of figures and infographics; his handwritten notes; publish early on Leanpub and LinkedIn, then on Amazon KDP, possibly launched in Portugal around Web Summit (Lisbon, 9 to 12 November 2026).

## The workflow

- **A new repository for the edition** (name to agree with Dinis; it is not created by this brief), where the agentic workflow is mapped as it runs: briefs, debriefs, the review findings, the interview transcripts, the chapter map and the builds.
- **Roles, one folder each,** after graphs.sgit.ai's team (librarian, researcher, writer, editor, developer, QA, publisher): at least a reviewer, an editor and a publisher. Reuse the shelf, reading pages and book-as-graph tooling from graphs.sgit.ai rather than rebuilding them.
- **Step 1, a thorough review of the 2018 text:** spelling, facts that dated, broken links, and a verdict per chapter (keep, rewrite, cut, merge). One record per chapter, the way this site's reviews work.
- **Step 2, the editor interviews Dinis.** The method of *ten hard questions for RiskMandate*: the agent acts as the book's editor, asks one question at a time, challenges each answer, and keeps going until the narrative is coherent. Interviews are by voice memo or in a session; transcripts kept verbatim in the repository. Opening questions for the first interview:
  1. Who exactly is the reader you hand this to, and what do they do differently the week after reading it?
  2. The 2018 cover says "question everything". What should a reader question about AI that the 2026 conversation does not?
  3. Where does a new developer start: as an explorer, a villager or a town planner? Can they choose?
  4. You say young Europeans are fed fear about AI. Fear of what, from whom, and what is the honest answer to it?
  5. Which 2018 chapter are you most proud of, and which would you cut?
  6. "Everyone is a developer" can sound like a slogan. What can't someone who only briefs agents do, that a 2018 developer could?
  7. What is the first workflow the reader should run, end to end, on day one?
- **Step 3, the chapter map and drafts:** from the review, the interviews and the newsroom articles. Figures and infographics for every chapter.
- **Step 4, quick iterations in public:** versions on Leanpub, posts on LinkedIn, feedback taken into the next version.
