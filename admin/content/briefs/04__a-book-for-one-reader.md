## In short

  * **An agent can assemble the book.** Once it is a graph, at every altitude.
  * **So it can assemble an edition for one person.** Their chapters, their altitude, their examples, later their language.
  * **Sent to them, not published.** A PDF made for them.
  * **The brief is private.** It comes from my correspondence notes, and stays there.
  * **Pay as you read.** Start with five pounds; the more you read, the more you pay.
  * **It starts in the browser.** And can be kept in the reader's vault.



## An edition for one

I love this idea. The more the book becomes a semantic graph, the more it can be assembled programmatically, by an agent, even a cloud session, from its parts. [A website to finish the book](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/a-website-to-finish-the-book.html) makes the book a graph, at every altitude, with the narrative as a path through it. An agent that can walk that path can walk a different one for one person.

So I could say: here is this person. They are a designer moving into product, they think in pictures, they are worried about AI taking their job. Make them their edition. The agent picks the chapters that matter to them, at their altitude, uses examples from their world, puts the infographics first, and, eventually, writes it in their language and with their culture in mind. It is the same idea as sending someone a newsletter written for them, applied to a whole book.

## The brief is already there

In a way, the brief for each person already exists. The agents that run my correspondence, [the agent team as it runs](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-agent-team-as-it-runs.html), keep a CRM that is one folder per person, with what we talked about and where each fact came from. That is exactly what an editor would want to know before choosing what to give someone to read.

Which makes the privacy rule important. That folder is private, and it stays private. The agent reads it to make the choices, and the edition carries the result, not the notes: no private detail about the person, nothing they told me in confidence, nothing they would be surprised to see. The edition is sent to them, not published. And when [the directory of people and their keys](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/a-directory-of-people-and-keys.html) exists, it can be encrypted to their key, so only they can open it.

## Pay as you read

Then the part that made me say yes out loud: let people pay for the book as they read it, the way this newsroom already charges for articles. [Paying after the value](https://newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/paying-after-the-value.html) describes the experiment: you start with five pounds of credit, each piece you read uses a few pence, you can set the price from free to double, and the balance can go below zero without blocking you.

For the book, the same principle: start with five pounds. The more of the book you read, the more you pay. And it should work for the personalised edition too, because the meter counts what you read, whichever edition you read it in. It starts locally, in the reader's browser, as the meter does today; and there should be the option to save it, and the reading, in the reader's own vault.

## How it would work

  1. **The book as a graph** , at every altitude, on the book's website.
  2. **A brief for one reader** , written privately from my notes, or chosen by the reader on a form: their role, their altitude, their language.
  3. **An agent assembles the edition** from the graph: the chapters, the order, the altitude, the examples, the infographics.
  4. **A PDF, or the edition in the browser** , sent to that person only.
  5. **The meter** , starting with five pounds of credit, charging for what is read.
  6. **The reader's vault** , if they want to keep the edition and their reading.



## How innovation works

This is also an example of how I think innovation works. I now know the book's website is going to exist, and I know how we build things here: vaults, graphs, the meter, agents with roles. So I can already build on top of it, in my head and in a brief, before it exists. Each thing we build becomes something the next idea stands on.

## What this does not claim

  * **Nothing is built.** Not the website, not the graph, not the editions.
  * **Personal editions are sent, not published.** And the private notes behind them stay private.
  * **Translation and culture come later.** The first editions would be in English, at an altitude, with examples chosen.
  * **The meter is an experiment.** Whether people pay for a book this way is what we would find out.
