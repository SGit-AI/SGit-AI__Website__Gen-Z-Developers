Gen ZX Developers: a proposal to make this the GenAI startup edition, for Generation Z and Generation X, with one objective, that by the end of the book the reader has started a company

By Dinis Cruz, co-written with this site’s journalist, gen-z.journalist, in Dinis Cruz’s voice; the editorial responsibility is Dinis Cruz’s.

Abstract. The more I thought about who I want to give the first version of this book to, the more I arrived at the founder. So I am proposing a change, as the first set of changes to the first release candidate, which is now built: the tagline becomes the GenAI Startup Edition, and the book is for two generations, Generation Z, who are entering the workforce and are the new entrepreneurs, and Generation X, who are the subject-matter experts I see starting companies, the people I work with, my own generation, and often the parents of Generation Z. The millennials in the middle can benefit from it, but theirs is a different story. Two generations together make Generation ZX, which is a lovely plot twist, because it goes back to my origins, the ZX Spectrum. And they are developers: they are coding, with agents, the explorer coding way, which is the chapter that should replace vibe coding. That gives the book an objective: to empower the reader to create a startup by the end of it. So it should say how to actually do it, for real: create a company in the UK, the case for Lithuania, Portugal, the European Union, the cloud providers and the startup programmes, and start selling. Ship now, and ship serverless: no servers, no databases, client side, with SG/Send and sgit for data privacy and RiskMandate's techniques to control the agents. And we measure the book's success by how many companies are created from it, which means asking people to tell us what they are doing, and building a community around it. This article maps what would change, chapter by chapter, and which newsroom articles we would bring in that we are not covering now.

In short

  • The reader I want to give this book to is the founder. This is a proposal, the first set of changes to the book being made now.
  • The tagline: the GenAI Startup Edition. Because what the reader builds is a GenAI startup; it would change the title decided this morning, Gen Z Developers (GenAI Edition).
  • Two generations, not one: Generation Z and Generation X. The ones entering the workforce, and the subject-matter experts starting companies; often their parents.
  • Together they are Generation ZX. A plot twist that goes back to my origins: the ZX Spectrum.
  • They are developers. Coding with agents, the explorer coding way, not vibe coding.
  • The book has an objective: by the end, you have started a company. For real: the company, the cloud, the programmes, the first sale.
  • Ship now, and ship serverless. No servers, no databases, client side; SG/Send and sgit for privacy; RiskMandate's techniques for control.
  • We measure the book by the companies created from it. And build a community of the people who tell us.

Who I want to give it to

The more I was thinking about who I want to give the first version of this book to, who the target audience is, and what they should be doing, the more I arrived at, fundamentally, the founder. Not founder as an attitude: the 2018 book had a chapter called Be a founder, and what it meant was "act like one of the founders of the business" you work for. That is still good advice. But in 2026 the advice is literal. You can be the founder.

So I want to propose a change, and I want it to be the first set of changes to the release candidate we are making now (the book in release mode). The first release candidate is built and readable, rc1: thirty chapters in nine parts, 50,607 words. This article maps what would change in it, so that the board can hear it and I can decide, and rc2 can be written under the frame I choose.

It would also change the title. This morning I named the book Gen Z Developers (GenAI Edition) (decision 010). The proposal is Gen ZX Developers, with the GenAI Startup Edition as the tagline. A decision changes by another decision, so that is mine to make after the hearing, not something this article does.

Generation X, too

The people that fit the startup mode very well, the people I am working with, and also me, my generation, so it is also a book for me, are Generation X. So I am thinking that we should make this book for both Generation Z and Generation X.

A lot of the entrepreneurs I see are from Generation X. They are the subject-matter experts. That is the argument of the expert founders: "Execution just got cheap." The person who has spent twenty years inside a problem can now build the first version of the answer themselves, with agents, instead of explaining it to a team they could never afford. And Generation Z is the new generation, which is hungry and focused on this, entering the workforce and becoming the entrepreneurs, and tends to be the kids of Generation X, which is an interesting gap.

That leaves the millennials in the middle. Of course they can benefit from this book, but I think theirs is a different story. These two are who we go for.

Generation ZX

And this takes us to another cool thing. If we are talking about Generation Z and Generation X, we actually have Generation ZX. Which is a lovely plot twist, because it goes back to my youth, to my origins: the ZX Spectrum. So it is actually really cool to talk about Gen ZX developers, because that is what they are. The entrepreneurs I am talking about are developers. They are coding, they are vibe coding, they are doing explorer coding the way we do.

Explorer coding, not vibe coding

That is another chapter that should be here, instead of vibe coding, though we should explain the move from vibe coding and why we think it is explorer coding. The newsroom's explorer coding says it in one line: "What people call vibe coding is exploration, and the word should say so." The explorer builds to find out, and what the exploration produces is the brief for the version other people will rely on; who will fix your mess is the handover. A founder needs both halves.

The objective: start a company

If you think about it, this gives the book an objective: for somebody to create a startup. Our job is to empower the reader to create a startup by the end of the book. This can become the book you give somebody who wants to start a company: do you want to start a GenAI company? Here is the book for you to read.

So it should contain how to actually do it, for real. What the steps are. Creating a limited company in the UK. A chapter about Lithuania. Portugal, because that is where I am from. A chapter on the European Union and what exists there, based on our experience of how it works. The cloud providers that exist, the startup funds and the startup programmes that exist right now. We can be the champions for the UK, and maybe Portugal, and eventually other countries.

And then start selling. The whole thing is about shipping now. As the question is whether they miss it puts it: "Not to plan, not to pitch, not to architect. Ship." You ship it, and it is painful. If you give it away and they do not miss it, you try again.

Ship serverless

So in a way what we should say is: ship your startup serverless. We should bring in the articles about no databases, no servers, all client-side solutions, because they are very relevant: a founder with no servers has nothing to run at three in the morning and nothing to pay for when nobody is using it. No server by design is careful about what that means: "It is no servers of mine with logic in them." And ephemeral town planner services has the test: "If it bills you by the hour when nobody is using it, it is not serverless."

Then SG/Send and sgit come in, because they provide a piece of the puzzle from a data privacy point of view: data encrypted on the user's device, on a server that cannot read it. And RiskMandate, as the techniques to control the agents your company runs on, which the book already covers in controlling your agent.

Measured by the companies it creates

I think we should say it: we measure the success of this book by how many companies have been created from it. Which is quite a cool thing to do. It means we ask people to contribute, and to tell us what they are doing, and we try to create a community around it. A book that is a book to read is fine. A book with an objective is a different thing.

What the newsroom does not have

These need new articles, one memo of mine each, before their chapters can be written:

  • Creating a limited company in the UK. No newsroom article covers it.
  • Lithuania. Two lines so far, in not a straight line: I am looking at it for the next company, "for its digital advantages"; nothing is registered yet.
  • Portugal. Only that my Portuguese company is being closed, because running a company with little revenue there is expensive in a way it is not in England. The Portuguese ecosystem graph, pt.newsroom.sgit.ai, is the place to start.
  • The European Union: its programmes and what exists for a founder, from our experience.
  • The first customers, beyond pricing.
  • The community, and the measure: how a reader tells us about their company, and what we do with it.

Precisely

  • The generations, by the dates most often used (the Pew Research Center's): Generation X born 1965 to 1980, millennials 1981 to 1996, Generation Z 1997 to 2012. Boundaries differ between sources; the book should say which it uses.
  • The ZX Spectrum was launched by Sinclair Research in the UK in April 1982, when Generation X was between two and seventeen.
  • It is Gen ZX, not Gen XZ. XZ is also the name of the compression library that had a backdoor planted in it in 2024, the most famous supply-chain attack of recent years; in a book that covers security, the order of the letters matters.
  • The expert founders, in numbers, from the newsroom's article: a third of new US startups now have a single founder; in a study of 2.7 million US founders, the mean age at founding of the fastest-growing one in a thousand new companies was 45.

Questions for me, kept apart:

  • The title. Decided: Generation ZX Developers (GenAI Startup Edition), decision 014. Still open: is it the second edition of Generation Z Developers, or a new book that grew out of it?
  • The millennials: does the book say why it is not for them first, or leave it unsaid?
  • Lithuania and the EU: written now, from what I know, or after I have done it?

  • The cast: Marta, a Generation X expert founder and Zara's mother, proposed for the storyboards.

What this does not claim

  • This is a proposal. Nothing in the chapter map, rc1 or the title changes until the board has heard it and I have decided.
  • "A book for Generation X and Generation Z" is about who it is written for first, not who may read it.
  • The startup programmes and credits change every year. That is why they go in the hands-on part, dated.
  • The company chapters are from experience, mine and the people I work with, not legal or tax advice, and the book should say so.
  • "Creating a limited company in the UK" is how this article reads the memo's words on the UK chapter.

If you started a company because of something you read here, tell me: write to agent@riskmandate.ai. That is the first entry in the measure.
