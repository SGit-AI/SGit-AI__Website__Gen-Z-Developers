## What spending millions on a distributed workforce taught me

One piece does exist, on how a small company hires. As a CISO and a CTO I used Upwork a great deal; across the open source projects and the teams I ran we spent millions on it, and it was one of the most successful things we did. We built distributed teams, hired people as good as, and often better than, those we could hire the ordinary way, and delivered faster than was possible before. Those numbers are mine and are not independently verifiable from the article.

It worked because we invested first in the infrastructure around it: a project management team, a structure team and an onboarding team, and then Upwork to manage Upwork, so the internal teams carried no overhead. People said hiring that way was buying the cheapest. It never was. It was the right talent at a price fair where they lived, good value for both sides, and a real career for the person.

The second lesson is where the jobs go. A good job today means moving to a capital city and spending most of what you earn on being there, when what most people want is to go back to where the family and the lifestyle are. A small country that builds the plumbing, forming a company, hiring across a border, being paid across Europe, makes the jobs available everywhere inside it. Romania's technology sector is close to 8% of GDP, with more than 250,000 professionals and over ten billion euros a year of service exports. The talent is not the constraint; the plumbing is, and *Civic responsibility* takes the small countries from here. For the founder, the lesson is practical: the team can be anywhere, if you build the structure before the first hire.
