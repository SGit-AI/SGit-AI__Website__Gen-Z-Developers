About this release candidate

This is the first end-to-end draft of Gen Z Developers (GenAI Edition), the second edition of the 2018 book, and it was not written the way the first edition was. In 2018 Dinis wrote the chapters himself, in a public repository, and readers sent fixes as GitHub issues. In 2026 the text in your hands was written in a day, by the book's agents, from a register of ideas that Dinis had already said in his own words.

Here is how that works, so that you know what you are reading.

Since the first edition, Dinis has talked the second one into existence: voice memos, written up with a journalist agent as articles in his voice, each ending with a brief for the roles that finish a book (a reviewer, an editor, a publisher, a librarian). The librarian read every article, every decision and every one of the 2018 chapters and outlines, and wrote one idea per thing the book would say: a sentence the book stands behind, with where it came from, down to the section of the article, and the 2018 file it answers. One hundred and six ideas, at the time of this draft. Fifty of them are marked accepted, because Dinis said them as his own position in a co-written article; the rest are the librarian's reading of the material, waiting for him.

The ideas were placed on a proposed chapter map: thirty chapters in nine parts. Then each chapter was written from the ideas placed in it, by an agent that read the articles and the 2018 text behind them and nothing else. The rule was simple: every number, every quotation and every story in a chapter has to be in a source the chapter names. Where the sources ran out, the chapter says so, in a note addressed to Dinis. There are a few dozen of those notes; the back matter collects them. They are the questions only he can answer: a story only he knows, a decision the articles left open, a number he has to check.

So this is a candidate, not a version. The first edition is still the only official one. This text is frozen, so that what Dinis reads and marks up cannot change behind him, and his feedback will produce the next candidate rather than an edit of this one. Nothing will be published from it outside the website where it lives. Every chapter ends with where it came from, as links, and the website shows the whole graph: which articles were processed into which ideas, which ideas went into which chapter, and what each article's brief asked for and whether it was done.

The voice is Dinis's throughout, in the first person, because the material is his: his memos, his articles, his 2018 book. Where a chapter sounds like him, that is the material doing its work. Where it does not, that is the agents', and it is exactly what the review is for. The verbs Dinis uses to answer are the ones the book's process gives him: keep, rewrite, merge, cut, article first, later, not doing it. Each one, on an idea or on a chapter, becomes a decision recorded in the book's vault, and the next candidate carries it.

One thing is decided and one is not. The title is Gen Z Developers (GenAI Edition): Dinis chose it on 19 October 2026, while this draft was being written, closing a question the articles had kept open. Whether the hands-on part, which is dated 2026 and meant to be renewed each year, lives inside the book or as a companion is still his; it is included here so that the decision can be made by reading it.

Read it as a reader first. Then, if you are Dinis, read it with a pen.
