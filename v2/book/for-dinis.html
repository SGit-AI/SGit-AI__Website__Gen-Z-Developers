Everything that waits on Dinis

The release candidate was written from the register, and the register was written from the articles; where the articles stop, the chapters stop and say so. This page collects every For Dinis note in the 30 chapters, and the ideas the map has not placed.

A release candidate, not a version. This is v2.0.0-rc1 of the second edition: every chapter written by the book's agents from the ideas register, for Dinis to read, print and mark up. Nothing in it is signed off. The first edition, v1.0.0, is the only official version; the candidate's text is frozen at v2/versions/v2.0.0-rc1.md so that what was read cannot change behind the reader (decision 009).

The notes in the chapters (34)

  • You are lucky to be here: who was the podcast? If you can find it, the book should credit her here; the article left her unnamed.
  • You are lucky to be here: does the dot-com crash belong here, on the first pages, or later in the book? The article left it as your question. It is here for now because part of the reader's fear is a fear of exactly this crash.
  • Everything is connected: graphs: the 2018 outline's first bullet says "See my presentation on Graphs". Which presentation did you mean? If it still exists, the chapter should name it and take from it.
  • Everything is connected: graphs: graphs before Wardley maps, or the other way round? The chapter map puts this one first because a map is a graph with positions; the article leaves the order as your question.
  • Where do you land?: three examples of jobs that never scaled, and now can. The article asks for them and the chapter needs them here; without them this section is a claim without a case.
  • Connect the dots backwards: your own dots, connected backwards. The article asks which three make the best story; this is where they go.
  • Connect the dots backwards: what does multidisciplinary look like for an explorer, a villager and a town planner? The article asks it as an interview question; one example each would anchor this chapter for the three readers.
  • What generative AI is: the book can only reproduce Lee Brimelow's image with his permission. The alternative the article names is to extend *The Starry Night* ourselves, with a named tool, and show that. Which one?
  • What generative AI is: is Gen Alpha a reader of this edition, or of the next one? The other half of the name question, still for the title interview.
  • Lenses, roles and loops: the first time you saw a model ask for a tool instead of answering. The article's interview script asks for it, and it would open this section better than the dates do.
  • Everyone who can brief an agent is a developer: the article plans "what the briefing-only developer cannot do" as interview question 6, and the material gives only the non-functional requirements list. Your own answer, with one example of a brief that was not enough, belongs here.
  • Explorer coding, and the handover: the newsroom calls explorer coding a proposal, and the ideas register still has it as a candidate. Is it the book's word, used throughout, or does the book say "what people call vibe coding" and explain it once?
  • Hired for the workflow: the OWASP interview: which project, roughly which year, and whether the company can be named. The chapter stands without it, but the story is better with the detail.
  • Act as the job you want: the person at the startup summit: may she be named, and what happened next? The article says you have not asked her yet.
  • Act as the job you want: the memo on the job hunt in 2026: interviews, take-home tests with and without AI, a junior role day to day, freelancing. *Start from the storyline* and the survey both list it as the first gap; this section is the placeholder for it.
  • Publish, publish, publish: your own newsroom: the 2018 outline promised a graph of how much great minds and artists publish, as evidence that frequency is a sign of talent. Did you have a source in mind, or should that claim be cut?
  • Your first workspace: the beginner's checklist of accounts and what they cost (the Workspace seat, the Claude plan, the agent's own mailbox) is still a gap in the material; the only figure the articles give is "a few pounds a month" for a Workspace mailbox.
  • How to work with agents: when the article was written the Librarian did not run yet; the sources existed and the brief was written. Does it run now, and what did the first weeks of briefs change about how you start the day?
  • Direction is the scarce skill: the Google executive's test appears in the 2018 outline and again in *How cheap is it to undo*, both times from memory; if the source can be found the book should name it, and if not, say so once.
  • Facts, evidence and the record: the cost figure is from your memory; the article asks the book to give one example of such a system with a real number, which only you can supply.
  • Facts, evidence and the record: two of the article's questions for you are left open here: how you prepare for a meeting today, step by step; and whether the book may mention your daughters, as the article does when it makes the Snapchat point. This draft keeps them out until you decide.
  • Controlling your agent, and security for the agent era: the 2018 outline on third-party modules asked for the story behind the developers who thought of mining coins from users with the sleep function, and a simulated npm hack story. Only you know whether either belongs here.
  • Open source: your own first pull request to someone else's project, and what it led to; the 2018 chapter says the usual outcome is nothing, and a reader will want the counter-example from your life.
  • Building blocks: what a newcomer like Priya should expect to pay in a month, for one site, one vault and a model on a free tier with their own key; the register says costs for a newcomer is still a gap, and only you have the receipts.
  • Shipped means the user has it: the story of the site that went down for hours over one character, a pipe, in an nginx config; the 2018 TDD outline promises it and this is where it belongs.
  • Graphs, memory and your future self: the 2018 *Future Self* outline promised a Twitter case study from the writing of that book, with two screenshots and no text. What did they show? The story would make this section concrete.
  • Be a founder: the article discloses that two of the three expert founders are people you help, and that sgit.ai and RiskMandate are yours. Does the book carry that disclosure in the chapter text, as here, or only in the provenance the build renders?
  • Civic responsibility: the register notes that a Portuguese edition, or both languages, is your open question, and this chapter is the one it bears on most. Which is it, and does the Hacking Portugal material stay here or move to the new edition of that book?
  • An agentic workflow from zero: the walkthrough is written for someone with customers, and asks for three real contacts and the topic the person sells to be substituted. For a reader looking for a first job, should the exercises be rewritten around their own contacts?
  • A tiny agent loop: the article asks for "a page of code or a no-code version". This is the no-code version. The page of code needs your choice of language and tool, and whether it lives on the page or on the site, where it can be kept current.
  • Images, infographics and storyboards, drawn by code: the Guide's pronouns are they/them in the cast file until you decide how the author appears. Is the Guide drawn as you?
  • Meeting briefs and follow-ups: the article asks you for how you prepare for a meeting today, step by step. This chapter needs it: the three briefs above are the shape, and your steps are the chapter.
  • Meeting briefs and follow-ups: "hundreds of thousands or millions of pounds" is from your memory of what such systems cost. The article says the book should give an example with a number.
  • How this book was made: two readings of your memos are recorded as the journalist's, for you to confirm: that "V2.0" meant site v0.2.0 as the start of the working copy, and that the hands-on part belongs inside the book rather than as a companion renewed every year.
