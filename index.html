Generation Z Developers

Key concepts and ideas for the next generation of developers.

  • Question everything
  • Make fact based decisions
  • Understand the history of tech
  • Learn how to learn
  • Understand what game is being played
  • Develop intellectual curiosity
  • Look under the hood
  • Shake things up

The eight lines on the cover are what the book was for. They read more urgently now than in 2018, and the second edition keeps all eight: the manual for getting into development, and getting a job, now that everyone who can brief an agent is a developer.

This site's articles

How the work is briefed: each idea, task or review is written up as an article first, then a role is pointed at it.

  1. Write it as an article: how I brief the agents that finish this book, and why their first job is to ship the book we already shipped
  2. Release the book we already released: v1.0.0 end to end, built in this repository, matched page by page to the PDF Leanpub made in 2018, and every format from the same source
  3. One board for the book: every issue a file, a Kanban page built from the files, and every content issue pinned to a version and a sentence
  4. Everything unfinished in one place: the outlines, the drafts, the 2018 GitHub issues and my handwritten notes, as one inventory I can read through and decide on
  5. Read, comment, decide: how I go through the pile, one item at a time, and how the editor turns my decisions into the next version of the book
  6. Start from the storyline: what to bring from the newsroom's 204 articles, the topics and concepts the second edition should cover, and the book as storyboards drawn by code, to flick through before a chapter is written
  7. You are lucky to be here: the core idea of the second edition is wonder, not fear, because whoever reads it is living through the biggest change of their career while the pieces are still landing, and can finally make technology work for them
  8. Where do you land? Wardley maps run through the whole book, shown before they are named, and the first thing a reader should work out is their centre of gravity: explorer, villager or town planner
  9. Connect the dots backwards: your path only makes sense looking back, the edge is now in mixing disciplines, learning is a lifelong skill you can finally shape to how you think, and the printed page is still the best technology to learn from
  10. Where the thinking came from: what the book takes from my influences register, influence by influence, where each one lands, and what the 2018 book had already written down about them
  11. Generative AI, not AI: the technology this book is about is the one that generates, how I understand it works, from next-word prediction to the image that made it click, and where I was corrected
  12. Follow the rabbit holes: provenance, explainability and asking for the evidence are the skills that matter most now, this generation is already good at them, and generative AI makes it cheap to be the best-prepared person in the room
  13. Everything is connected: a graph is a network of nodes and edges, not a diagram; a map is a graph with positions, provenance is a graph, the world is groups of groups, and you can start anywhere
  14. Lenses, roles and loops: a prompt is a lens that changes who the answer is for, a role is a model with a description, an agent is a loop with a goal, tools are requests the model sends back, and long chats forget because they are compressed
  15. The book in release mode: a first release candidate from the list we have, then rounds, where every idea, change and word that wants to come in argues its case before an adversarial review board, and every verdict becomes a precedent that makes the next one faster
  16. Gen ZX Developers: a proposal to make this the GenAI startup edition, for Generation Z and Generation X, with one objective, that by the end of the book the reader has started a company
  17. Dinis's first decisions: the licence, a PDF built here for Leanpub and Amazon, every format from one source, text issues in the vault, minor by default, and a second stream of articles for the agents
  18. Storyboards drawn by code, and the newsroom read in full: how I surveyed 204 articles for the book, built a renderer that turns a JSON storyboard into sketched, animated panels, and what the first two storyboards show
  19. The book as a graph: how I read eleven articles, seven decisions and a survey into a register of ideas with their provenance, proposed a chapter map, and made the build refuse a dangling edge
  20. The release candidate, written from the register: how thirty chapters of Gen Z Developers (GenAI Edition) were drafted in a day from the ideas, the articles and the 2018 text, frozen, and handed to Dinis with the questions only he can answer
