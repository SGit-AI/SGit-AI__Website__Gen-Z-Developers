The team

The roles that finish the book, one folder each, after the seven roles of graphs.sgit.ai and the agent team as it runs. The book's plan asks for a reviewer, an editor and a publisher at least; each reads the book, gives feedback, challenges it, writes interview scripts for Dinis, and signs off its part. Their briefs, debriefs, issues and interview transcripts are kept here, in the repository, so the workflow is mapped as it runs.

role

The review board

A book grows by things that were each a good idea on their own. The only defence is that every addition has to win against someone whose job is to say no, in front of the readers it is for, with its sources checked; and that each answer is written down once, so it is not argued again.

The editor

The book's voice is Dinis's, and the way to get it is to make him say it out loud and defend it. The method is the one used for ten hard questions for RiskMandate: an agent as the interviewer, every answer challenged, for as long as it takes.

The illustrator

An infographic in this book shows a structure the text already carries: a loop, a test, a sum, who can read what. Drawn from data, it stays correct when the text changes, and the build can check it against the chapter word for word; drawn by hand or by an image model, it drifts the day the chapter is edited. So the default is code, every number and quoted line on a drawing is checked against its source, and an image model is used only where a mood matters more than a structure, with its brief kept beside the image.

The journalist

An instruction written as an article is better than an instruction written as a list. Writing it forces the research before the work starts: what already exists in this repository and the vault, what the 2018 book actually said, what the counts are, what is a decision for Dinis and what is not. The role that receives it starts from the record, not from a guess, and Dinis can read it, correct it and say "yes, that".

The librarian

"Which articles have we used" cannot be answered by remembering; it is answered by an edge. A source is processed when an idea cites it, a 2018 file is answered when the map or an idea names it, and an idea is in the book when a decision in the vault and a signed-off version say so. The graph is the record of the publishing process, and the build refuses a dangling reference.

Every role names what it refuses. A role definition that would read the same in any other repository has failed its brief. The roles' working copies, the board and the reviews-in-progress live in the public vault; what is signed off comes back here.

The shape is the one graphs.sgit.ai uses: role.md with Identity, Foundation, Primary Responsibilities and Core Workflows; issues/open|blocked|done/ where the folder is the status; briefs/ and debriefs/.
