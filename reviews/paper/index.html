  • The first pages sell the book (8): The opening is about the reader, and the reason to read comes first (Zoom in, not discover).
  • Show the book, do not list it (13): A story or a picture where an argument would do less.
  • Doing starts early, and runs through (3): A manual, not a journey: you learn by doing, so you start doing early (Zoom in, not discover).
  • Topics first, details under them (14): Detail the point does not need is cut (Zoom in, not discover).
  • The making-of out of the reader's way (15): The 2018 book and its outlines go to the section at the end.
  • The voice is mine (32): Lines that sound odd, hedged or not like Dinis are rewritten.
  • Meet the reader where they are (21): Explain a term before using it, with a short analogy, and say it first, then link.
  • A release, not a draft (7): The release candidate is shipped and sold; it should read as a book, not as a working document.
  • Fact or opinion, visibly (5): The reader can see which claims are backed by data and which are Dinis's opinion, and the data is found (Follow the rabbit holes).
  • The website holds what the page cannot (5): Where the book needs more room, a QR code sends the reader to the site: the source, the explainer, the visualisation.
  • What works, keep it (9): The ticks: what the pen approves is kept, and the format it likes is used again.
  • Answers to For Dinis (2): Where the candidate asked Dinis a question in a For Dinis box, the pen answers it, and the next candidate acts on the answer.
  • Draw it (9): Where an idea is a shape (a graph, a map, an evolution, a comparison), the book draws it instead of describing it.
