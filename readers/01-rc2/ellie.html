Ellie Marsh (invented)

Generation Z, 26. “Can someone like me, who has never coded, actually build and sell this, and will this book show me how without making me feel stupid?”

Everybody in this study is invented. The readers, their names and every word attributed to them were written by a language model (Claude Sonnet, one agent per reader) reading the candidate's text. It is not reader research, and no sentence in it is evidence about a real person or a real market. What is evidence is the candidate's own text: every quote a reader cites is checked by the build, word for word, against the unit it cites, in the frozen file whose hash is recorded here.

Who they are

situation
Account manager at a small marketing agency in Bristol. Has never written code. Uses ChatGPT for drafts at work. Has an idea she has been sitting on for a year: a booking and reminders tool for independent dog groomers, because her sister is one and runs the business from a paper diary and WhatsApp.
arrives with
Can someone like me, who has never coded, actually build and sell this, and will this book show me how without making me feel stupid?
knows
Marketing, customers, how small businesses buy, social media, using a chat assistant.
does not know
What an agent is beyond a chat window, servers, databases, serverless, graphs, open source, what a repository is.
decides on
Whether it feels written for her, whether there is something she can do this weekend, and the reviews.
leaves when
Jargon piles up without explanation, it assumes she codes, or it turns out to be a book about the author's products.
patience
medium: reads in the evening on her Kindle, a chapter at a time; will push through one hard chapter, not two in a row
format
ebook on a Kindle
reads like
Reads in order, every word of a chapter that speaks to her, skims the ones that do not, highlights lines to come back to.

Nearest in the storyboard cast: priya.

What we expected, before the run

hypothesis
Chapter 8 (everyone who can brief an agent is a developer) is the chapter for her, but she meets graphs, generative AI's insides and lenses first, and the serverless and privacy chapters assume vocabulary she does not have.
expected path
about → ch:1 → ch:2 → ch:3 → ch:4 → ch:5 → ch:6 → ch:7 → ch:8 → ch:9 → skim part 3 → ch:13 (her first workspace) → part 6
expected put down
in The frame (ch:6 or ch:7), or in Build it without a server (ch:23 to 25) if she gets that far
expected buy
maybe: she wants it to be for her, and she will say so if it is not
watch for
Undefined jargon (agent, serverless, repository, graph); whether there is a weekend task she could do; whether SG/Send, sgit and RiskMandate read as advertising.

The run has not happened yet. The journey above was written first, on purpose.
